How Often Should Aged Care Carpets Be Cleaned?

Every facility manager inherits a cleaning schedule from somewhere: the previous FM, the cleaning contractor's standard offer, or whatever survived the last budget round. Few of those schedules were designed around how the carpet in each zone actually gets used. Here's a more useful way to set the cadence.

Published July 2026 · Carpet Cleaning · Maintenance

There Is No Single Right Answer, But There Is a Right Method

The honest answer to "how often" is: it depends on the zone, the traffic, and what lands on the carpet in between. A corridor between the dining room and the lounge takes hundreds of walker and wheelchair passes a day. A bedroom in a low-acuity wing might take twenty. Cleaning both on the same cycle means one of them is being cleaned too often, and the other not nearly often enough.

Before cadence, though, it's worth being clear about what periodic cleaning is for. Vacuuming removes dry soil. Periodic professional cleaning removes what vacuuming can't: the oily film that dulls carpet fibre, the bonded soil that grinds pile down over time, and the organic residues that feed the odour problems we covered in our article on why aged care floors develop that smell. Get the periodic programme right and the carpet looks better, smells neutral, and lasts years longer. Skip it and no amount of daily vacuuming saves the asset.

A Sensible Cadence by Zone

As a starting framework, most aged care facilities land somewhere close to this, then adjust with experience:

  • Main corridors, lounges, and dining-adjacent carpet: every three to six months. These zones carry the traffic, the food spills, and the visible soiling, and they're also the areas visitors and assessors see first.
  • Resident bedrooms: every six to twelve months as a baseline, with reactive cleans in between for incontinence incidents and spills. Rooms of residents with continence needs usually justify a shorter cycle.
  • Admin areas, offices, and low-traffic wings: annually is often enough, provided vacuuming is consistent.
  • Entry areas and thresholds: match the corridor cycle, and check the entrance matting while you're at it. Good matting extends every other cycle in the building.

Two things should override the calendar. The first is soiling you can see or smell: carpet never benefits from waiting for a scheduled date. The second is the manufacturer's warranty. Commercial carpet warranties from the major manufacturers generally require a documented maintenance programme with periodic professional cleaning by an approved method. Keep the records, because they do double duty as evidence in your accreditation file, something we cover in our article on flooring under the strengthened Quality Standards.

Why the Method Matters as Much as the Frequency

Aged care rules out some methods that are fine elsewhere. Hot water extraction done properly leaves carpet damp for hours, and in a building where residents need corridors back and where trapped humidity feeds odour, hours of drying time is a real cost. Done badly, it leaves moisture in the underlay, and wet underlay in aged care is how odour and wicking stains start.

This is why we run low-moisture encapsulation as our standard method for aged care carpet. The chemistry is a healthcare-safe polymer that surrounds soil particles and crystallises as it dries, so the soil releases from the fibre and comes out in the next vacuum pass. It's completely odourless, and carpet is dry and walkable in around 15 to 30 minutes, which means bedrooms and corridors come back online inside the same shift rather than the next morning. On sites where encapsulation isn't practical, we use hot water extraction instead, but it's the exception, not the default. The full method breakdown is on our carpet cleaning page.

One honest caveat about scope: our cleaning service is carpet only. We don't do hard floor stripping, polishing, or strip-and-seals, so if your facility needs a vinyl maintenance programme, that sits with your general cleaning contractor.

Who Should Do the Cleaning?

There are two ways to run a periodic carpet programme in aged care, and we offer both. The first is the conventional model: our crews attend on the agreed cycle and do the work. The second is one we're unusually keen on: we train your existing general cleaner in the encapsulation method, supply the chemistry, and let your in-house team run the routine cycles, with our crews handling the periodic deep cleans and anything unusual. It earns us less per facility, and we still think it's often the right call, for reasons our Special Projects Director explains in his article on training general cleaners.

Whichever model fits your facility, the marker of a good programme is the same: a written schedule per zone, records of every clean, and carpet that never gets the chance to reach visibly dirty. Premrest runs these programmes for aged care facilities across Melbourne, Sydney, and Brisbane, and the wider commercial carpet cleaning and flooring business operates nationally through premrest.com.au.

The Bottom Line

Set the cadence by zone rather than by building: three to six months where the traffic is, six to twelve months where it isn't, and reactive cleans whenever incidents happen. Choose a low-moisture method so cleaning never takes floor space away from residents for longer than a coffee break. Then write it all down, because the same records that protect your carpet warranty also feed your accreditation evidence file.
