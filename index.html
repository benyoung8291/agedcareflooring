Why Aged Care Is Different

A floor that passes at handover isn't the same as a floor that keeps passing.

Aged care flooring is not a generic commercial floor with a healthcare colourway. Nursing homes and retirement villages need carpet tiles and vinyl specified to AS 4586, set out to dementia-friendly guidance, and installed while residents are still in the building.

Most flooring problems in aged care don't show up on installation day. They show up twelve months later: the vinyl that met its slip rating on day one fails a pendulum test after a year of alkaline mopping, or a resident with dementia stops at a dark threshold strip because it reads as a step down.

An aged care floor has several jobs to do at once. It has to hold its AS 4586 slip rating under a real cleaning regime, not just in the product data sheet. It has to support infection control with seamless, coved, cleanable surfaces. It has to follow dementia-friendly design guidance so residents can navigate confidently. And it has to stand up to wheelchairs, walkers and meal trolleys every day for a decade.

Getting that right means specifying against AS 4586:2013, AS 4663:2013, the NSQHS Standards and the Aged Care Quality Standards on every project — and handing over the test reports and certifications your accreditation audits will ask for.

It also means planning around the reality that residents live in the building while the work happens. We stage installations wing by wing, work after hours where it helps, and agree the sequence with your care team before anyone lifts a tile.

Low-Moisture Encap Carpet Cleaning

Odourless carpet cleaning with a 15–30 minute dry time

Most aged care carpets that look exhausted aren't worn out — they're just badly cleaned. We use the encapsulation (encap) system: a polymer detergent worked into the carpet with a counter-rotating brush. The polymer locks soil into brittle crystals that vacuum out next service. Less moisture, no odour, no wet floors blocking corridors during medication rounds. Carpet only — we don't do hard floor stripping, polishing or strip-and-seals.

Standards & Compliance

The standards we specify against

Five frameworks shape aged care flooring in Australia. We specify against all of them on every project, and hand over the documentation with the job.

AS 4586:2013

Slip resistance classification for pedestrian surfaces. The standard that determines whether your floor is compliant. We specify products with verified P-ratings for every area type.

AS 4663:2013

In-situ slip testing using the wet pendulum method. We test installed floors to confirm they actually perform, not just what the product data sheet says, but what your floor does in the real world.

HB 198:2014

The guide to slip resistance that covers wayfinding, contrast, and design for vulnerable populations. Essential reading for dementia-friendly environments.

NSQHS Standards

National Safety and Quality Health Service Standards require infection control through seamless, cleanable surfaces. This drives our vinyl specification approach.

Aged Care Quality Standards

The standards that govern safety, quality of life, and care outcomes in aged care. Appropriate flooring directly supports physical safety and cognitive comfort.

Questions facility managers ask us

Who supplies and installs aged care flooring in Australia?

Premrest is a specialist aged care flooring contractor. Do you do aged care carpet installs?

Aged care carpet installs are a core part of our aged care flooring work: Interface and Shaw Contract carpet tiles in bedrooms, corridors and common areas, including tile-level replacement when a single tile is stained or worn, staged around live operations so the facility stays open. We also install vinyl in wet areas, dining rooms and clinical spaces. Both are specified to AS 4586. Melbourne, Sydney and Brisbane.

Do you supply and install vinyl planks as well as sheet vinyl?

Yes. Luxury vinyl planks (Karndean, Armstrong, Tarkett) for bedrooms, lounges and corridors where a warm timber look matters; heat-welded sheet safety vinyl for bathrooms, kitchens and clinical areas where infection control requires a seamless, coved finish. Both specified to AS 4586.

What flooring is best for aged care facilities?

It depends on the area. Carpet tiles (Interface, Shaw Contract) work best in bedrooms, corridors and common areas — quiet, dementia-friendly, and replaceable tile by tile. Vinyl is ideal for wet areas, dining rooms and clinical spaces where infection control and slip resistance are critical.

What slip resistance standards apply in Australia?

AS 4586:2013 for slip resistance classification and AS 4663:2013 for in-situ pendulum testing. Most aged care areas require P3–P5 ratings — and they must hold up after months of cleaning and use, not just at handover. We test and rectify, not just install.

Can flooring be replaced without relocating residents?

In most cases, yes. We stage installations around your operations: after-hours and weekend work, wing-by-wing rollouts, advance subfloor preparation, and coordination with your care team. Where a zone does need to be cleared during works, we plan it together in advance so any move is short and happens once. The facility stays operational throughout.

What is dementia-friendly flooring?

Flooring that follows HB 198:2014 and dementia care research: calm matte colours with appropriate contrast for wayfinding, no bold patterns or dark strips that read as holes or obstacles, and consistent tones that reduce anxiety. Read our dementia design guide →
